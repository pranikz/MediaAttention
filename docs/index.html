01 · Why attention

An ad that isn't seen can't sell.

Attention is the first gate in advertising: the brand, product and message have to be looked at before they can persuade or be remembered. Decades of eye-tracking research show it can be predicted, and that small design choices move it a lot.

Size and placement steer the eye

In magazine-ad eye tracking, pictures capture attention regardless of size, text gains in proportion to its surface, and attention on the brand transfers to the rest of the ad.

Pieters & Wedel, 2004 [17]
Saliency biases choice

When people decide quickly, products that are simply more visually salient get chosen more often, even over ones they prefer.

Milosavljevic et al., 2012 [19]
Complexity cuts both ways

Varied, well-organised design helps an ad stop people and get liked; dense, cluttered detail hurts both.

Pieters, Wedel & Batra, 2010 [18]
02 · What is what

Seven numbers, each with a job.

Every metric below says what it measures, how MediaAttention computes it, and what to do about it. Nothing is a black box: the formulas are in the code and the README.

01

Attention heatmap & fog

PIXEL MAP
What
Where viewers are predicted to fixate during the first seconds of free viewing. The fog map is the inverse: whatever stays fogged is likely missed.
How
DeepGaze IIE [1], an ensemble of deep networks trained on human fixations (SALICON [4], MIT1003 [5]), with a centre-bias prior [7].
Do
If your logo or offer sits in fog, move it, enlarge it or give it contrast.
02

Focus

0–100 · HIGHER = SHARPER
What
Whether attention concentrates on a few elements or scatters across the frame.
How
The share of the image needed to hold 80% of predicted attention, inverted to 0–100: an attention-dispersion measure as used in TV-ad eye tracking [20].
Do
Low focus means competing elements. Cut one, or build a clear visual hierarchy.
03

Cognitive demand

0–100 · MODERATE IS BEST
What
How much visual information a viewer has to process.
How
Mean Shannon entropy [15] of local 16×16 blocks, a simple clutter measure in the spirit of [16]. Per frame for video.
Do
Too high: simplify copy and background. Too low: there may be nothing to hold the eye.
04

AOI attention & lift

% OF ATTENTION · LIFT ×
What
The share of attention on your brand, product, headline, body copy and CTA, plus lift: attention share ÷ area share.
How
Text roles come from OCR (PP-OCR models via RapidOCR [9]) and layout rules; logo and product come from open-vocabulary detection with OWLv2 [8].
Do
Brand under ~5% of attention is a branding risk. A CTA with lift under 1 is being overlooked.
05

Memory

0–100 · OPTIONAL
What
The predicted share of viewers who would recognise the creative after a brief exposure.
How
ResMem [10], trained on the LaMem [11] and MemCat [13] recognition datasets, building on the memorability work of [12].
Do
Compare variants against each other within your corpus; absolute values cluster high.
06

Impact score

1–10 · ≥7 READY
What
One pre-flight number that rolls up the metrics that matter.
How
Each metric scores 10 inside its target range and drops linearly outside; the impact score is the mean. Targets come from your own ads (below).
Do
≥7 launch · 3.5–6.9 optimise · ≤3.4 rework. Read the per-metric scores to see why.
07

Video: cuts, openings, endings & tracking

PER FRAME · TIMELINE
What
Every metric over time; hard cuts; focus and memory in the first and last two seconds; attention on each AOI overall, while it's on screen, and how long it's visible.
How
Frames decoded with PyAV, cuts found from histogram jumps, attention smoothed over the last second so it lingers after a cut like real gaze. AOIs are detected at each shot start and tracked with pyramidal Lucas–Kanade optical flow [21, 22, 23].
Do
Don't land the brand or offer right after a hard cut; memory dips there (the "doorway effect" [24]). Check the brand is on screen early and long enough.

03 · How it's built

Open models, honest maths.

MediaAttention stitches proven, openly published models together with transparent metrics. Each stage is small enough to read in one sitting and swap out when something better appears.

  1. Predict attention

    A fixation-density map for the whole frame, calibrated to how people actually look.

    DeepGaze IIE [1] · spectral-residual fallback [3]

  2. Read the layout

    OCR finds every line of text; size and button-shaped patches sort it into headline, body and CTA.

    RapidOCR / PP-OCR [9]

  3. Find brand & product

    Open-vocabulary detection scores each role separately, so the logo can't steal the button's box.

    OWLv2 [8]

  4. Score

    Focus, cognitive demand and per-AOI attention are pure numpy over the heatmap; memory is a separate network.

    numpy · ResMem [10]

  5. Follow video

    Decode every frame, detect cuts, smooth attention over a one-second window, track AOIs within each shot.

    PyAV · OpenCV LK flow [21–23]

  6. Calibrate

    bench scores your own ads and sets each target to the top 20% (middle 50% for complexity).

    percentiles · per category

  7. Roll up

    Per-metric 1–10 scores against those targets, averaged into the impact score, skipping what's absent.

    impact_score()

  8. Show it

    Heatmap, fog and AOI overlays, a heatmap video, a timeline, JSON for pipelines, and a local web UI.

    Pillow · Gradio [25]

04 · Calibrate

Scored against your own best work.

Generic targets lie: a luxury OOH poster and a TikTok ad shouldn't chase the same complexity. Point bench at 30+ ads from one category, and every metric's target becomes the band your top performers hit. Do this once per industry or use case and score every new creative against it.

1 · Collect

One folder per category: FMCG social, auto OOH, fintech display…

2 · Bench

Command line or the web UI's Calibrate tab. You get benchmarks.json and per-asset scores.

3 · Score new work

Pass the targets with --benchmarks, or let the UI load them automatically.

05 · Get started

Three commands.

Python 3.10+. Works on CPU, NVIDIA GPUs and Apple Silicon. Model weights download on first use.

Install

git clone https://github.com/pranikz/MediaAttention
cd MediaAttention
pip install -e ".[deepgaze,aoi,video,ui]"
# optional, non-commercial:
pip install -e ".[memory]"

Web UI

mediaattention ui
# → http://localhost:7860
# Analyse tab: drop an ad
# Calibrate tab: drop a batch

Command line

mediaattention ad.png --aoi auto \
  --product "a running shoe"
mediaattention spot.mp4 --aoi auto
mediaattention bench ads/fmcg/ \
  --aoi auto --out bench/
06 · Honest limits

What it won't tell you.

Predictions are a fast first filter, not a substitute for testing with real people when the stakes are high.

  • Trained on natural images, not ads. The attention model learned from public eye-tracking datasets; it can be less sharp on text-heavy creatives.
  • No emotion scores yet. Engagement, trust and intent need human response data that hasn't been released openly. They're on the roadmap, not faked.
  • Layout rules, not a layout model. A headline smaller than its body copy, a ghost button or a centred logo can fool AOI detection; pass your own boxes then.
  • Targets are only as good as your corpus. bench ranks ads by their own scores; rank by real KPIs (CTR, recall) when you have them.
  • Memory scores cluster high and ResMem's weights are non-commercial. It's an optional extra for that reason.
  • Video has no audio. Sound, voice-over and music drive attention in video and aren't modelled.
