Why attention

Attention is the first gate in advertising. The brand, the product and the message have to be looked at before they can persuade or be remembered. Decades of eye-tracking research show attention can be predicted, and that small design choices move it a lot.

Pieters & Wedel, 2004 [17]

Size and placement steer the eye

In magazine-ad eye tracking, pictures capture attention regardless of size, text gains in proportion to its surface, and attention on the brand transfers to the rest of the ad.

Milosavljevic et al., 2012 [19]

Saliency biases choice

When people decide quickly, products that are simply more visually salient get chosen more often, even over ones they prefer.

Pieters, Wedel & Batra, 2010 [18]

Complexity cuts both ways

Varied, well-organised design helps an ad stop people and get liked; dense, cluttered detail hurts both.

What is what

Seven outputs, each with a job. For every metric: what it measures, how MediaAttention computes it, and what to do about it. The formulas are in the code and the README.

01 · pixel map

Heatmap & fog

WHERE PEOPLE LOOK
What
Where viewers are predicted to fixate in the first seconds of free viewing. The fog map is the inverse: whatever stays fogged is likely missed.
How
MediaAttention-Ads v1: the compact UNISAL network [26], retrained on eye tracking of posters, ads and interfaces (UEyes [27]). On held-out posters it beats every open model we tested. Stock UNISAL and DeepGaze IIE [1] are optional alternatives.
Do
If your logo or offer sits in fog, move it, enlarge it or give it contrast.
02 · 0–100, higher is sharper

Focus

CONCENTRATED OR SCATTERED
What
Whether attention lands on a few elements or scatters across the frame.
How
The share of the image needed to hold 80% of predicted attention, inverted to 0–100: attention dispersion, as used in TV-ad eye tracking [20].
Do
Low focus means competing elements. Cut one, or build a clearer visual hierarchy.
03 · 0–100, moderate is best

Cognitive demand

HOW MUCH THERE IS TO PROCESS
What
How much visual information a viewer has to take in.
How
Mean Shannon entropy [15] of local 16×16 blocks, a simple clutter measure in the spirit of [16]. Per frame for video.
Do
Too high: simplify copy and background. Too low: there may be nothing to hold the eye.
04 · % of attention, lift ×

AOI attention & lift

BRAND · PRODUCT · HEADLINE · BODY · CTA
What
The share of attention on each element, plus lift: attention share ÷ area share.
How
Text roles come from OCR (PP-OCR models via RapidOCR [9]) and layout rules; logo and product from open-vocabulary detection with OWLv2 [8].
Do
Brand under ~5% of attention is a branding risk. A CTA with lift under 1 is being overlooked.
05 · 0–100, optional

Memory

WILL THEY RECOGNISE IT
What
The predicted share of viewers who would recognise the creative after a brief exposure.
How
ResMem [10], trained on the LaMem [11] and MemCat [13] recognition datasets, building on the memorability work of [12].
Do
Compare variants against each other within your corpus; absolute values cluster high.
06 · 1–10, ≥7 ready

Impact score

ONE PRE-FLIGHT NUMBER
What
A roll-up of the metrics that matter for the creative.
How
Each metric scores 10 inside its target range and drops linearly outside; the impact score is the mean. Targets come from your own ads.
Do
≥7 launch · 3.5–6.9 optimise · ≤3.4 rework. Read the per-metric scores to see why.
07 · per frame, timeline

Video: cuts, openings, endings & tracking

EVERYTHING ABOVE, OVER TIME
What
Every metric over time; hard cuts; focus and memory in the first and last two seconds; attention on each AOI overall, while it's on screen, and how long it's visible.
How
Frames decoded with PyAV, cuts found from histogram jumps, attention smoothed over the last second so it lingers after a cut like real gaze. AOIs are detected at each shot start and tracked with pyramidal Lucas–Kanade optical flow [21, 22, 23].
Do
Don't land the brand or offer right after a hard cut; memory dips there (the "doorway effect" [24]). Make sure the brand is on screen early and long enough.

How it's built

Openly published models stitched together with transparent maths. Each stage is small enough to read in one sitting and swap out when something better appears.

  1. attention

    Predict attention

    A fixation-density map for the whole frame, calibrated to how people actually look.

    MediaAttention-Ads v1 · UNISAL [26] · DeepGaze IIE [1] · spectral residual [3]
  2. layout

    Read the layout

    OCR finds every text line; size and button-shaped patches sort them into headline, body and CTA.

    RapidOCR / PP-OCR [9]
  3. objects

    Find brand & product

    Open-vocabulary detection scores each role separately, so the logo can't steal the button's box.

    OWLv2 [8]
  4. metrics

    Score

    Focus, cognitive demand and AOI attention are numpy over the heatmap; memory is a separate network.

    numpy · ResMem [10]
  5. video

    Follow video

    Decode every frame, detect cuts, smooth attention over one second, track AOIs within each shot.

    PyAV · OpenCV LK flow [21–23]
  6. bench

    Calibrate

    Score your own ads; each target becomes the top 20% (middle 50% for complexity).

    percentiles per category
  7. impact

    Roll up

    Per-metric 1–10 scores against those targets, averaged, skipping what's absent.

    impact_score()
  8. outputs

    Show it

    Heatmap, fog and AOI overlays, a heatmap video, a timeline, JSON and a local web UI.

    Pillow · Gradio [25]
attention model · MediaAttention-Ads v1, built on UNISAL (Droste et al., ECCV 2020)
how to read it

One network, images and video

  • Encoder: a MobileNetV2 reads the ad at three scales. Sixteen learned Gaussian maps add a prior for where people tend to look first.
  • Bypass-RNN: for video, a ConvGRU carries attention from frame to frame, so it lingers after a cut like real gaze. For still images it is skipped.
  • Decoder: upsamples twice, mixing in finer encoder features through skip connections, so small text and buttons stay sharp.
  • Domain heads: batch norm, adaptation and a learned 41×41 smoothing kernel are specific to each training set: SALICON for images, DHF1K for video.
  • Output: a log-softmax over pixels, a probability map of where the next fixation lands.

Our model: MediaAttention-Ads v1

Public attention models learned from photos of streets, animals and faces. Ads are type, logos, buttons and layout. So we retrained a compact network on eye tracking of posters, ads and interfaces (UEyes [27]). On held-out posters it matches human attention 52% better than the best other open model, at a fraction of the size.

How it was trained

The UNISAL [26] network was started from its SALICON weights and fine-tuned on the 1,872 UEyes training images, with 10% held out to choose the epoch. The loss was KL divergence − CC against the fixation density, with AdamW at lr 3e-5 and batch norm frozen. Training took 8 epochs (~25 min on an M3 Pro), and held-out CC rose from 0.502 to 0.769. The test split and FiWI were never seen, and FiWI CC still rose from 0.616 to 0.631, so it generalises rather than memorises. Video keeps the stock UNISAL video branch.

where we win
  • Ads, posters and UIs. First in every UEyes category, by 0.19–0.26 CC.
  • Text-heavy layouts. Best of all models on FiWI's text pages (0.597).
  • Size and speed. 20–120× smaller; a 1,000-ad batch in under a minute on a laptop.
  • One network for images and video.
  • Licence. Apache-2.0 end to end; the DeepGaze models have no licence file.
where we don't (yet)
  • Photo-led creatives. DeepGaze MSDB [29] is still ~0.05 CC ahead on FiWI and further ahead on pictorial pages.
  • Video is not ad-tuned. There is no open ad-video eye-tracking set yet.
  • One panel. UEyes is 62 participants in one country, free viewing in a lab. A one-second glance in a feed is different.

Your own best work

Generic targets lie: a luxury OOH poster and a TikTok ad shouldn't chase the same complexity. Point bench at 30+ ads from one category, and every metric's target becomes the band your top performers hit.

Your own panel

Every commercial tool rests on a private panel. MediaAttention ships the panel kit: a browser study you send to anyone, with no server and no uploads. Webcam eye tracking, gut-reaction word tests and a memory test give you real attention, engagement, trust and memory data on your own ads.

1 · describe

Write study.json

The ads, a few unseen "foil" ads for the memory test, and the words to react to: Engaging, Trustworthy, Boring…

2 · share

Send one link

Participants calibrate with 9 clicks, watch each ad for 5 s, then answer Yes/No with F and J, timed in milliseconds. They download a results file at the end.

3 · import

Get real data

mediaattention collect turns the files into an eye-tracking dataset to score models against, plus per-ad engagement, trust, avoidance, intent and memory scores for bench --kpi.

where the data lives

Nowhere central: there is no server and no database. During the study, results exist only in the participant's browser tab. Webcam frames are processed on the spot and discarded, so no video or image of anyone is ever stored or sent.

At the end, the participant downloads one results file (gaze coordinates, answers and reaction times; no name, email or IP, just a random ID) to their own device. They choose whether to send it to you, and you import it locally. The study page itself is static: nothing flows back to it.

Gaze comes from MediaPipe face landmarks (Apache-2.0) and a per-person calibration. Expect 2–4° of error, which is fine for heatmaps across many people. Try the study with your webcam, or without a camera (your mouse stands in for your eyes).

Honest limits

Predictions are a fast first filter, not a substitute for testing with real people when the stakes are high.

  • One lab panel. The image model is tuned on UEyes: 62 participants, free viewing, a lab eye tracker. Feed glances are shorter and busier. Video attention is not ad-tuned yet.
  • No emotion scores yet. Engagement, trust and intent need human response data that hasn't been released openly. They're on the roadmap, not faked.
  • Layout rules, not a layout model. A headline smaller than its body copy, a ghost button or a centred logo can fool AOI detection; pass your own boxes then.
  • Targets are only as good as your corpus. bench ranks ads by their own scores; rank by real KPIs (CTR, recall) when you have them.
  • Memory scores cluster high, and ResMem's weights are non-commercial, which is why it's an optional extra.
  • Check model licences. The default model, MediaAttention-Ads v1 (a UNISAL fine-tune on UEyes, CC BY 4.0), is Apache-2.0. The optional DeepGaze IIE's repository has no licence file (all rights reserved by default) and ResMem is non-commercial. Both are downloaded from their authors at runtime, never redistributed here.
  • Video has no audio. Sound, voice-over and music drive attention in video and aren't modelled.
